    Why can't the browser tell me more? ▾

    1. User-Agent Reduction & Freezing

    Modern browsers intentionally freeze parts of navigator.userAgent to curb passive tracking. For example, Safari locks macOS to 10_15_7 and omits iPhone/iPad model identifiers entirely, while Chrome replaces detailed Android build/model tokens with a generic K unless high-entropy Client Hints are requested over HTTPS.

    2. Fingerprinting Protection & Bucketing

    Browsers like Firefox (with Enhanced Tracking Protection) and Brave spoof or coarsen high-entropy signals—such as capping navigator.deviceMemory to 8 GB, rounding hardwareConcurrency, or reporting a standardized viewport/timezone—so websites cannot uniquely identify your machine across sessions.

    3. Physical Dimensions Are Hidden by Design

    Web standards expose logical CSS pixels and devicePixelRatio, but never physical millimeter/inch dimensions of your display panel. Without knowing the physical diagonal size (or querying the permission-gated Screen Details API on multi-monitor desktop setups), true hardware PPI cannot be computed automatically.
